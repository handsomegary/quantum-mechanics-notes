\notepage{48}{Total angular momentum and ladder coefficients}
For general angular momentum, use $\mathbf J$ instead of the orbital
symbol $\mathbf L$:
\[
 J^2\ket{j,m}=j(j+1)\hbar^2\ket{j,m},\qquad
 J_z\ket{j,m}=m\hbar\ket{j,m}.
\]
For a spinless particle, $\mathbf J=\mathbf L$ and $j=\ell$, recovering
the orbital formulas.

Suppose
\[
 J_\pm\ket{j,m}=C_\pm^{jm}\ket{j,m\pm1},\qquad J_-=J_+^\dagger.
\]
Taking the adjoint of the raising equation gives
\[
 \bra{j,m}J_-=(C_+^{jm})^*\bra{j,m+1}.
\]
For normalized eigenstates,
\begin{align*}
 \abs{C_+^{jm}}^2
 &=\mel{j,m}{J_-J_+}{j,m}\\
 &=\mel{j,m}{J^2-J_z^2-\hbar J_z}{j,m}\\
 &=\hbar^2\bigl[j(j+1)-m^2-m\bigr]\\
 &=\hbar^2(j-m)(j+m+1).
\end{align*}
Choose the relative phases so that these coefficients are real and
nonnegative. The analogous calculation for lowering yields
\[
 C_+^{jm}=\hbar\sqrt{(j-m)(j+m+1)},\qquad
 C_-^{jm}=\hbar\sqrt{(j+m)(j-m+1)}.
\]
Therefore,
\[
 \boxed{J_\pm\ket{j,m}
 =\hbar\sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}\ket{j,m\pm1}.}
\]
\smallheading{Exercise retained from the notes}
Calculate $\Delta L_x(t)$ and $\Delta L_y(t)$.
\editorial{A time-dependent answer requires a state and Hamiltonian.
For a state $\ket{\ell,m}$ evolving only by an overall energy phase,
$\langle L_x\rangle=\langle L_y\rangle=0$ and
$\Delta L_x=\Delta L_y=\hbar\sqrt{[\ell(\ell+1)-m^2]/2}$.
This states the assumptions rather than assigning an unspecified
time evolution.}
