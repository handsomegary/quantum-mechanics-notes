\notepage{62}{Spin and orbital operators on two-component wavefunctions}
The three Pauli matrices are
\[
 \sigma_x=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 \sigma_y=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\ii\\\ii&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 \sigma_z=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad S_i=\frac\hbar2\sigma_i.
\]
Spin acts only on the two spin labels. In components,
\[
 \mel{\mathbf r,m_s}{S_i}{\Psi}
 =\sum_{m_s'}\mel{m_s}{S_i}{m_s'}
                \braket{\mathbf r,m_s'}{\Psi}.
\]
In column notation this is simply
\[
 (S_i\boldsymbol\Psi)(\mathbf r)
 =\frac\hbar2\sigma_i
 \begin{bmatrix}\Psi_+(\mathbf r)\\\Psi_-(\mathbf r)\end{bmatrix},
\]
where
\[
 \Psi_+(\mathbf r)=\braket{\mathbf r,+\tfrac12}{\Psi},\qquad
 \Psi_-(\mathbf r)=\braket{\mathbf r,-\tfrac12}{\Psi}.
\]

\smallheading{Orbital operators are diagonal in spin space}
For instance,
\[
 (L_z\boldsymbol\Psi)(\mathbf r)=
 \begin{bmatrix}
  -\ii\hbar\partial_\phi&0\\
  0&-\ii\hbar\partial_\phi
 \end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}\Psi_+(\mathbf r)\\\Psi_-(\mathbf r)\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Likewise $L^2$, $L_x$, $L_y$, and $L_\pm$ each act by the corresponding
orbital differential operator on both components, without mixing
the spin labels:
\[
 (A_r\otimes I_s)\boldsymbol\Psi
 =\begin{bmatrix}A_r\Psi_+\\A_r\Psi_-\end{bmatrix}
 \quad\text{for }A_r\in\{L^2,L_x,L_y,L_z,L_+,L_-\}.
\]
