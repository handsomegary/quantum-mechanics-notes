\notepage{61}{Fixed spin and normalization of a spinor wavefunction}
In the spin-up/down basis, the entries of $S^2$ are
\[
 \begin{bmatrix}
  \mel{+}{S^2}{+}&\mel{+}{S^2}{-}\\
  \mel{-}{S^2}{+}&\mel{-}{S^2}{-}
 \end{bmatrix}
 =\hbar^2\begin{bmatrix}
  \tfrac12(\tfrac12+1)&0\\
  0&\tfrac12(\tfrac12+1)
 \end{bmatrix}
 =\frac34\hbar^2I_s.
\]
Thus $S^2$ acts as the same constant on every state of the
spin-one-half representation. More generally, intrinsic spin labels
an elementary particle through the eigenvalue $s(s+1)\hbar^2$, in the
same sense that charge and rest mass are intrinsic labels. In
contrast, the orbital quantum number $\ell$ can vary between spatial
states of the same particle.

\smallheading{Normalization in space and spin}
Insert the complete product basis into $\braket{\Psi}{\Psi}$:
\begin{align*}
 1&=\braket{\Psi}{\Psi}\\
  &=\int\dd^3r\sum_{m_s=\pm1/2}
     \braket{\Psi}{\mathbf r,m_s}
     \braket{\mathbf r,m_s}{\Psi}\\
  &=\int\dd^3r\sum_{m_s=\pm1/2}
     \abs{\braket{\mathbf r,m_s}{\Psi}}^2\\
  &=\int\left[\abs{\Psi_+(\mathbf r)}^2
                    +\abs{\Psi_-(\mathbf r)}^2\right]\dd^3r.
\end{align*}
Equivalently,
\[
 1=\int\boldsymbol\Psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)
          \boldsymbol\Psi(\mathbf r)\dd^3r
 =\int
 \begin{bmatrix}\Psi_+^*(\mathbf r)&\Psi_-^*(\mathbf r)\end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}\Psi_+(\mathbf r)\\\Psi_-(\mathbf r)\end{bmatrix}
 \dd^3r.
\]
\editorial{The adjoint is a row and the ket representation is a
column. Their order above gives a scalar norm; the reversed
column-times-row sketch in the source would instead give an outer
product matrix.}
