\notepage{16}{Commuting observables and functions of operators}
\smallheading{A complete set of commuting observables}
When a collection of mutually commuting Hermitian operators resolves
all remaining degeneracies, its simultaneous eigenvalues label the
joint eigenvectors unambiguously:
\[
 \Omega,\Lambda,\Gamma,\ldots
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ket{\omega,\lambda,\gamma,\ldots}.
\]
In the finite-dimensional setting, commuting operators can be added
to refine any remaining degenerate subspaces.

\smallheading{Power series}
Define $\Omega^n$ as the product of $n$ copies of $\Omega$, with
$\Omega^0=I$. If $f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nx^n$ converges in the
required domain, define
\[
 f(\Omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n\Omega^n.
\]
The most frequently used example is
\[
 \ee^{\Omega}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Omega^n}{n!}
 =I+\Omega+\frac{\Omega^2}{2!}+\frac{\Omega^3}{3!}+\cdots.
\]
If $\Omega\ket{\omega}=\omega\ket{\omega}$, then
\[
 \ee^{\lambda\Omega}\ket{\omega}=\ee^{\lambda\omega}\ket{\omega}.
\]
For a Hermitian $\Omega$ with an orthonormal eigenbasis,
\[
 \mel{\omega'}{\ee^{\lambda\Omega}}{\omega}
 =\delta_{\omega'\omega}\ee^{\lambda\omega},
\]
with additional Kronecker deltas for any degeneracy labels.

\smallheading{Differentiation and an operator equation}
For $\lambda$-independent $\Omega$,
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd\lambda}\ee^{\lambda\Omega}
 =\Omega\ee^{\lambda\Omega}=\ee^{\lambda\Omega}\Omega,
 \qquad \comm{\Omega}{\ee^{\lambda\Omega}}=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{\dd\Theta}{\dd\lambda}=\Omega\Theta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Theta(\lambda)=\ee^{\lambda\Omega}\Theta(0).
\]
\editorial{For an operator-valued initial constant, its position
matters. The exponential belongs on the left for this differential
equation. A scalar constant can be placed on either side.}
In general $\ee^{\Omega}\ee^{\Theta}\ne\ee^{\Omega+\Theta}$, but
\[
 \comm{\Omega}{\Theta}=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \ee^{\Omega}\ee^{\Theta}=\ee^{\Omega+\Theta}.
\]
