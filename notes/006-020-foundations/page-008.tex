\notepage{8}{Classes of operators, determinant, and trace}
A matrix is \emph{symmetric} if $A=A^T$. An operator is
\emph{Hermitian} if $\Omega^\dagger=\Omega$, and
\emph{anti-Hermitian} if $\Omega^\dagger=-\Omega$.

\smallheading{Analogy with complex numbers}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Property & Complex number & Operator\\
\midrule
Conjugation & $\alpha\mapsto\alpha^*$ & $\Omega\mapsto\Omega^\dagger$\\
Real / Hermitian & $\alpha^*=\alpha$ & $\Omega^\dagger=\Omega$\\
Imaginary / anti-Hermitian & $\alpha^*=-\alpha$ & $\Omega^\dagger=-\Omega$\\
Unit modulus / unitary & $\alpha\alpha^*=1$ & $UU^\dagger=U^\dagger U=I$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Every complex number splits into a real part and a purely imaginary part:
\[
 \alpha=\underbrace{\frac{\alpha+\alpha^*}{2}}_{\text{real}}
       +\underbrace{\frac{\alpha-\alpha^*}{2}}_{\text{purely imaginary}}.
\]
Similarly, every operator admits the decomposition
\[
 \Omega=
 \underbrace{\frac{\Omega+\Omega^\dagger}{2}}_{\text{Hermitian}}
 +\underbrace{\frac{\Omega-\Omega^\dagger}{2}}_{\text{anti-Hermitian}}.
\]
A \emph{unitary} operator satisfies
\[
 UU^\dagger=U^\dagger U=I.
\]

\smallheading{Basis-independent quantities}
In a finite-dimensional space, the determinant and trace are
\[
 \det\Omega=\det(\Omega_{ij}),
 \qquad \Tr\Omega=\sum_i\Omega_{ii}.
\]
Although the matrix entries depend on the basis, these two quantities
do not. The trace is invariant under cyclic permutations:
\[
 \Tr(\Omega\Lambda)=\Tr(\Lambda\Omega),
\]
\[
 \Tr(\Omega\Lambda\Theta)
 =\Tr(\Theta\Omega\Lambda)
 =\Tr(\Lambda\Theta\Omega).
\]
