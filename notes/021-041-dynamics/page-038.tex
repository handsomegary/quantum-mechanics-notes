\notepage{38}{Ladder Operators and the Ground-State Energy}
Define
\[
 a=\alpha X+\ii\beta P,\qquad a^\dagger=\alpha X-\ii\beta P,
 \qquad\comm a{a^\dagger}=I,
 \qquad H=\hbar\omega\left(a^\dagger a+\frac12\right).
\]
For convenience, use $\widehat H=H/(\hbar\omega)=a^\dagger a+1/2$ and
$\widehat H\ket\epsilon=\epsilon\ket\epsilon$. Then
\begin{align*}
 \comm a{\widehat H}
 &=\comm a{a^\dagger a}=\comm a{a^\dagger}a=a,\\
 \comm{a^\dagger}{\widehat H}
 &=a^\dagger\comm{a^\dagger}a=-a^\dagger.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 \widehat H a\ket\epsilon
 &=(a\widehat H-\comm a{\widehat H})\ket\epsilon
 =(a\widehat H-a)\ket\epsilon
 =(\epsilon-1)a\ket\epsilon,\\
 \widehat H a^\dagger\ket\epsilon
 &=(a^\dagger\widehat H-\comm{a^\dagger}{\widehat H})\ket\epsilon
 =(\epsilon+1)a^\dagger\ket\epsilon.
\end{align*}
Since this is a one-dimensional bound-state problem, nonzero states on
each rung are unique up to normalization:
\[
 a\ket\epsilon=B_\epsilon\ket{\epsilon-1},\qquad
 a^\dagger\ket\epsilon=C_\epsilon\ket{\epsilon+1}.
\]
The oscillator is confining:
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.7cm,y=0.4cm,>=Stealth]
 \draw[->] (-2,0)--(2,0) node[right] {$x$};
 \draw[ink,thick,domain=-1.6:1.6] plot (\x,{\x*\x});
 \node[anchor=west] at (2.1,1.7) {$V(x)=\frac12m\omega^2x^2$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
Could lowering continue indefinitely to $\epsilon-1,\epsilon-2,\ldots$?
For every normalizable state in the Hamiltonian's domain,
\begin{align*}
 \mel\psi H\psi
 &=\frac1{2m}\mel\psi{P^\dagger P}\psi
       +\frac12m\omega^2\mel\psi{X^\dagger X}\psi\\
 &=\frac1{2m}\norm{P\ket\psi}^2
       +\frac12m\omega^2\norm{X\ket\psi}^2\ge0.
\end{align*}
An indefinitely descending ladder would eventually have negative energy.
Hence there is a lowest state annihilated by $a$:
\[
 a\ket{\epsilon_0}=0\ \Longrightarrow\
 a^\dagger a\ket{\epsilon_0}=0\ \Longrightarrow\
 \widehat H\ket{\epsilon_0}=\frac12\ket{\epsilon_0},\qquad\epsilon_0=\frac12.
\]
