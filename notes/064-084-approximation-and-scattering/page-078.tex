\notepage{78}{One-dimensional scattering}
A stationary scattering eigenfunction of $H=T+V$ has an incident wave,
a reflected wave, and a transmitted wave. For a localized potential,
the asymptotic wave number is $k_0$ on both sides.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=0.7cm,>=Latex,font=\small]
 \draw[->] (-4.5,0)--(4.6,0) node[right] {$x$};

 --(4.1,0);
 \node at (0,1.6) {$V(x)$};
 \draw[->] (-3.9,1.8)--(-2.5,1.8) node[midway,above] {$A\ee^{\ii k_0x}$};
 \draw[->] (-2.5,.8)--(-3.9,.8) node[midway,below] {$B\ee^{-\ii k_0x}$};
 \draw[->] (2.2,1.8)--(3.8,1.8) node[midway,above] {$C\ee^{\ii k_0x}$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
The time-dependent picture uses wave packets:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Start with a normalized incident packet $\ket{\Psi}$ whose mean
       momentum is $\mel{\Psi}{P}{\Psi}=\hbar k_0$.
 \item Expand it in scattering eigenstates:
 \[
  \ket{\Psi}=\int a(k)\ket{\psi_k}\,\dd k,
  \qquad H\ket{\psi_k}=E_k\ket{\psi_k}.
 \]
 \item Evolve each energy component:
 \[
  \ket{\Psi(t)}=\int a(k)\ee^{-\ii E_kt/\hbar}\ket{\psi_k}\,\dd k.
 \]
 \item Long after scattering, the packet separates into reflected and
 transmitted parts:
 \[
  \ket{\Psi(t\to\infty)}=\ket{\Psi_R}+\ket{\Psi_T},\qquad
  R=\braket{\Psi_R}{\Psi_R}=\int\abs{\Psi_R(x)}^2\dd x.
 \]
\end{enumerate}

Equivalently, one solves the stationary problem:
\[
 \psi(x)=\begin{cases}
 A\ee^{\ii k_0x}+B\ee^{-\ii k_0x},&\text{left of the interaction region},\\
 \text{solution determined by }V(x),&\text{inside the interaction region},\\
 C\ee^{\ii k_0x},&\text{right of the interaction region}.
 \end{cases}
 \qquad R=\frac{|B|^2}{|A|^2}.
\]
\editorial{The stationary reflection coefficient is a ratio of
probability currents, hence squared amplitudes. The displayed formula
assumes the same asymptotic wave number on both sides.}
