\notepage{69}{Time-independent perturbation theory}
Suppose
\[
 H=H^0+H',\qquad
 H^0\ket{n^0}=E_n^0\ket{n^0},\qquad
 H\ket n=E_n\ket n.
\]
The unperturbed problem is solvable, while $H'$ is small compared with
the relevant scales of $H^0$. Expand the energy and state in orders of
the perturbation:
\begin{align*}
 \ket n&=\ket{n^0}+\ket{n^1}+\ket{n^2}+\cdots
        =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\ket{n^k},\\
 E_n&=E_n^0+E_n^1+E_n^2+\cdots=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E_n^k.
\end{align*}
A superscript $k$ denotes a $k$th-order correction, not a power. We hope
successive terms become smaller in the regime of interest.
\smallheading{Nondegenerate starting state}
Substitute the expansions into the exact eigenvalue equation:
\[
 (H^0+H')\bigl[\ket{n^0}+\ket{n^1}+\cdots\bigr]
 =(E_n^0+E_n^1+\cdots)\bigl[\ket{n^0}+\ket{n^1}+\cdots\bigr].
\]
Collecting equal orders gives
\begin{align*}
 \text{zeroth order:}\quad&H^0\ket{n^0}=E_n^0\ket{n^0},\\
 \text{first order:}\quad&H^0\ket{n^1}+H'\ket{n^0}
                   =E_n^0\ket{n^1}+E_n^1\ket{n^0}.
\end{align*}
Multiply the first-order equation by $\bra{n^0}$. Since
$\bra{n^0}H^0=E_n^0\bra{n^0}$, the terms containing $\ket{n^1}$ cancel:
\[
 \boxed{E_n^1=\mel{n^0}{H'}{n^0}.}
\]
